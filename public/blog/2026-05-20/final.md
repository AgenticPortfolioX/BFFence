## Do You Need a Permit for a Fence in Michigan?

This is the most common question we hear, and the answer depends entirely on where you live.

### Michigan's State-Level Framework

The Michigan Residential Code (based on the 2015 International Residential Code with state amendments) technically exempts fences under **7 feet tall** from requiring a building permit at the state level. However — and this is a big however — **individual cities and townships can (and do) impose their own stricter requirements.**

Most municipalities in Oakland County require some form of permit or zoning approval before you install a fence, even if the fence is under 7 feet. Some call it a "fence permit," others call it a "zoning compliance permit," and a few require nothing at all for standard residential fences.

The bottom line: **always check with your local building department before you start.** A permit costs anywhere from nothing to $100-$150, but building without one when it's required can lead to fines, forced removal, or headaches when you sell your home.

### City-by-City Permit Requirements in Oakland County

Here's a breakdown of what different Oakland County communities require:

**Auburn Hills** — Contact the Building Department at (248) 364-6949. Permits are handled through the Community Development Department. A zoning permit is typically required, and you'll need to submit a plot plan showing where the fence will go.

**Rochester** — The City of Rochester requires a **zoning permit** for all fences. According to the city's official requirements, fences must be in rear or side yards only (no front-yard fences), with a maximum height of 6 feet. Contact the Building Department for the application.

**Rochester Hills** — Fences along side and rear lot lines are allowed up to 6 feet in height. The city's zoning ordinance specifies that fences cannot extend closer to the front lot line than the front wall of the house. A zoning permit is required.

**Farmington Hills** — Residential fence permits cost **$100** and are processed through the Zoning Division (248-871-2520). You'll need to submit a completed application along with a survey or proposed plan. Farmington Hills even suggests getting your subdivision association's approval before submitting to the city to speed things up.

**White Lake Township** — Here's some good news: **residential properties in White Lake Township do NOT require a fence permit** for standard fences. The exception is pool fences, which must meet the International Swimming Pool and Spa Code barrier requirements. However, you still need to know your property lines and follow the township's Clear Zoning Ordinance.

**Troy** — Troy requires a fence permit through the Building Department. Standard residential height limits apply (4 feet front, 6 feet rear/side).

**Royal Oak** — Permits required. Royal Oak has specific material and height restrictions. The city is known for active code enforcement, so don't skip this step.

**Novi** — Fence permits required through the Building Department; property survey typically needed with application.

If your city isn't listed here, your best bet is to check your municipality's website or call their building department directly. A quick phone call can save you weeks of headaches.

## How Tall Can a Fence Be in Michigan?

The general rule across most of Michigan — and most Oakland County communities — is:

| Location | Typical Maximum Height |
|----------|----------------------|
| Backyard (rear yard) | **6 feet** |
| Side yard | **6 feet** |
| Front yard | **3 to 4 feet** |
| Corner lot sight triangle | **3 feet or less** |

### The "6-Foot Rule" Explained

The 6-foot maximum for rear and side yards is the most common standard in Oakland County. A 6-foot privacy fence is tall enough to give you real privacy in your backyard — blocking sight lines from neighbors and passersby — while still being short enough to avoid creating safety hazards or blocking essential sunlight.

**Important:** Some cities allow a rear yard fence up to 8 feet with a special permit or variance. Ann Arbor (just outside Oakland County but a useful comparison) allows rear yard fences up to 8 feet with a permit. Check with your local zoning office if you need extra height.

### Front Yard Height Limits

Front yard fences are almost always limited to 3 or 4 feet. This is a safety regulation — taller fences in front yards block drivers' views of children, pets, and oncoming traffic when pulling out of driveways. Some cities like **Dearborn prohibit front-yard fences entirely.**

### Corner Lot Sight Triangles

If you live on a corner lot, pay extra attention here. Most Oakland County municipalities enforce **sight triangle** requirements at intersections. Within this designated area — typically extending 30 feet back from the corner — fences cannot exceed 3 feet in height. This ensures drivers can see oncoming traffic before entering an intersection.

## Property Lines and Setback Requirements

### How Close to the Property Line Can You Build?

Michigan state law does not mandate a specific setback distance from property lines, but **most local ordinances require fences to be set back 2 to 6 inches inside your property line.**

Why inside your property line? Building directly on the line risks:

- **Encroachment** — If your fence ends up even an inch on your neighbor's side, you're looking at a legal dispute
- **Maintenance access** — You need room to stain, repair, and maintain both sides of the fence
- **Utility easements** — Many properties have underground utility easements that require access

### Why You Need a Property Survey

We cannot emphasize this enough: **get a professional property survey before you build.** We've seen too many homeowners rely on old fences, curb lines, or "best guesses" — only to find out later that their new fence sits on the neighbor's land.

A survey costs a few hundred dollars. A fence removal and relocation costs thousands, plus the legal fees if a dispute escalates. The survey is cheap insurance.

### What If Your Fence Is on the Property Line?

If you and your neighbor agree to place a fence directly on the property line, you're entering shared territory. In Michigan, a fence on the property line is generally considered shared property — both neighbors share maintenance responsibility. Get the agreement in writing. A friendly conversation today can prevent a bitter dispute tomorrow.

### Utility Easements and MISS DIG 811

Before you dig a single post hole, **call MISS DIG 811.** This free service (available statewide, including all of Oakland County) sends utility companies to mark underground gas lines, electric cables, water pipes, and communication lines on your property.

Most cities require proof of a MISS DIG request as part of the permit application. But even if yours doesn't, skip this step at your own risk. Hitting a gas line or underground electric cable is dangerous and expensive.

**Pro tip:** Take photos of the marked lines before you start digging. They fade over time and you'll want the documentation if there's ever a question.

## Michigan's Fence Viewers Act (What Most Homeowners Don't Know)

There's a Michigan law that dates back decades but is still on the books and actively used: the **Fence Viewers Act (Act 34 of 1978).**

Here's how it works:

- Each township must appoint at least one **fence viewer**
- If you have a dispute with a neighbor about fence cost-sharing or damage, you can request a fence viewer by paying **$25** to the township treasurer
- You must provide written notice to both parties at least 5 days in advance
- The fence viewer inspects the property and issues a **binding decision within 7 days**
- That decision can be recorded with the township clerk and applies to future property owners too

**What the Fence Viewer CAN do:**
- Determine cost-sharing for a fence used by both neighbors (based on current value, not original cost)
- Assess damage caused by animals

**What the Fence Viewer CANNOT do:**
- Settle boundary disputes (that requires a survey and potentially a court)
- Force a neighbor to pay for a fence they didn't agree to (for standard residential fences, the builder pays)

For most Oakland County homeowners, the Fence Viewers Act matters most when:
1. A neighbor's fence is damaged and they want you to share the repair cost
2. You're considering a shared fence and want a formal cost-sharing agreement

If you're planning a fence entirely on your own property, at your own cost, the Fence Viewers Act rarely comes into play. But it's good to know the option exists.

## HOA Restrictions: The Wild Card

Even if your city allows a 6-foot privacy fence made of cedar, your **Homeowners Association (HOA) can impose stricter rules.** And in Michigan, there's no state limit on how restrictive HOA fence rules can be.

### Common HOA Fence Restrictions in Oakland County:

- **Maximum height lower than city limits** (e.g., 4 feet instead of 6)
- **Approved materials only** (some HOAs ban chain link, others require vinyl)
- **Color restrictions** (white, black, or "natural wood tone" only)
- **Design standards** (no horizontal slat fences, board-on-board only)
- **Setback requirements** more strict than city rules
- **Prior written approval** required before installation

### The Golden Rule: Get HOA Approval in Writing

Before you spend a dime on materials or labor:

1. Get your HOA's architectural guidelines (CC&Rs)
2. Submit your fence plan for approval
3. Get the approval **in writing**
4. Keep that approval with your permit documents

We've seen homeowners get city permits, build a beautiful fence, and then be forced to tear it down because the HOA didn't approve the design. Don't let that be you.

## Material Restrictions and "Good Neighbor" Rules

### What Materials Are Allowed?

In most Oakland County communities, the following materials are permitted for residential fences:

- **Wood** (cedar, pressure-treated pine, redwood) — the most popular choice
- **Vinyl** — growing in popularity, low maintenance
- **Chain link** — allowed in most areas, though some suburbs limit it to 4 feet
- **Aluminum and wrought iron** — common for front yards and decorative fences
- **Composite** — newer option, higher cost but very durable

**Prohibited in all residential zones:**
- Barbed wire
- Razor wire
- Electrified fencing

### The "Finished Side Out" Rule

Most Oakland County municipalities (including Detroit, Flint, Grand Rapids, and many local suburbs) require the **finished side of the fence to face outward** — toward your neighbor's property or the street. This means the rails, posts, and structural supports should be on your side.

This isn't just about aesthetics. It's considered a "good neighbor" practice. A fence with the unfinished side facing outward looks sloppy and can reduce property values. Most quality fence contractors, including BF Fence, follow this standard automatically.

### Post Depth Requirements

Michigan's freeze-thaw cycle means your fence posts need to go deep enough to resist frost heave. The standard recommendation is:

- **At least 36 inches deep** for a 6-foot tall fence
- **Set in concrete** for stability
- **4-inch minimum diameter** for wood posts (or 1 5/8-inch for iron pipe)

A fence that isn't set deep enough will start leaning after the first Michigan winter. Don't cut corners here.

## Pool Fence Requirements

If your fence doubles as a pool barrier, the rules get stricter. While Michigan has no single state pool fence law, most municipalities follow the **International Residential Code (IRC)** standards:

- Barrier must be **at least 48 inches tall**
- No openings larger than a **4-inch sphere** (to prevent children from squeezing through)
- Gates must be **self-closing and self-latching**
- Gates must swing **away from the pool**
- Latch must be at least **54 inches above ground**
- No climbable elements on the exterior side

**Pool fences almost always require a permit** — even in townships like White Lake that exempt standard residential fences from permits.

## FAQs About Fence Permits and Regulations in Michigan

### Do I need a permit to replace an existing fence?

It depends. If you're replacing a fence in the same location, at the same height, with the same materials, many cities waive the permit requirement. But if you're changing the height, location, or design, a permit is usually required. Check with your local building department.

### Can my neighbor build a fence on the property line without my permission?

They can build on their side of the property line without your permission. If the fence goes on the actual property line, it's generally considered shared and both parties should agree. If it encroaches on your property, that's a legal matter that may require a survey and potentially legal action.

### How much does a fence permit cost in Oakland County?

Costs vary by municipality:
- **Farmington Hills:** $100
- **Rochester:** Varies (zoning permit fee applies)
- **White Lake:** $0 (no permit needed for standard residential fences)
- **Most cities:** $25 to $150 range

### How long does a fence permit take?

Simple fence permits in most Oakland County cities take **1 to 3 weeks**. More complex projects (commercial properties, unusual heights, historic districts) can take **4 to 8 weeks**.

### What happens if I build a fence without a permit?

Consequences vary but can include:
- Stop-work order (halt construction immediately)
- Fines (typically double the permit fee or more)
- Forced removal of the non-compliant fence
- Difficulty selling your home (unpermitted structures come up during home inspections)

Is skipping the permit worth the risk? In our experience, absolutely not.
