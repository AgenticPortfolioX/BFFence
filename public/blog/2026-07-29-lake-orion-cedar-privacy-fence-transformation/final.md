## Why Western Red Cedar? The Right Material for Michigan's Climate

When Lake Orion homeowners ask us what wood they should use for a privacy fence, we don't hedge. If the budget allows, the answer is Western red cedar.

Here's why.

### Cedar Handles Michigan Weather Naturally

Michigan's climate is brutal on outdoor structures. Here in Oakland County, we get about 35 to 45 inches of snow annually. Summer humidity regularly hits 80 percent. And every winter brings 40 to 60 freeze-thaw cycles that can warp, crack, and split ordinary lumber.

Western red cedar handles all of this because of what's inside the wood. The tree produces natural oils and resins called thujaplicins that act as built-in preservatives. These compounds protect the wood from rot, decay, and insect damage without any chemical treatment. Termites and carpenter ants — both of which are active in southeastern Michigan — tend to avoid cedar altogether.

Pressure-treated pine relies on chemical treatments like ACQ (Alkaline Copper Quaternary) to fight rot. When those chemicals leach out over time, the wood becomes vulnerable. With cedar, the protection is built into the wood's DNA. It doesn't wash away.

For the Wilsons, cedar made sense on every level. They wanted a natural look that would complement their Lake Orion property, and they wanted a fence that would still be standing strong when their kids were in high school.

### The Beauty Factor: Why Cedar Wins on Looks

Beyond durability, cedar is just a beautiful wood. It has a warm reddish-brown color with distinct, tight grain patterns. When the late afternoon sun hits a new cedar fence, it has a richness that transforms a yard.

One thing the Wilsons particularly loved was how cedar ages. Unlike pressure-treated pine, which tends to fade to a dull, blotchy gray after a single season, cedar weathers gracefully. If you leave it untreated, it develops an even, silvery-gray patina that many homeowners naturally prefer. The wood's structural integrity isn't compromised — it's purely an aesthetic change.

For the Lake Orion project, the Wilsons decided to let the cedar silver naturally. They liked the idea of a low-maintenance fence that would look established over time, like it had always been part of the property.

### Cedar Sets the Stage for Board-on-Board Construction

The choice of material also influenced the fence style. We recommended a board-on-board design, where pickets are staggered on alternating sides of the horizontal rails. This creates a completely solid barrier with no gaps — total privacy from every angle.

Board-on-board construction has several advantages over standard dog-eared privacy fences:

- **Zero sightlines** — no gaps that widen as wood shrinks over time
- **Equal beauty from both sides** — looks finished from inside the yard and from the street
- **Better wind resistance** — the staggered design allows some airflow while blocking visibility
- **Superior durability** — both sides of the pickets weather evenly, reducing warping

For a corner lot like the Wilsons' home, board-on-board was the ideal choice. The fence would be highly visible from the side street and the common area in back. It needed to look good from every angle.

## The Installation Process: From Consultation to Completion

### Step 1: Property Survey and Marking

Before we broke ground, we walked the entire property line with the Wilsons. We identified the boundary stakes, checked the survey, and marked the fence line with spray paint. For a Lake Orion corner lot, accurate property line identification is especially important since the fence runs along two public-facing edges.

We also discussed the important regulations. In Orion Township, privacy fences (those erected primarily for privacy or decoration) cannot exceed 6 feet in height and must not be located within any required yard setback area. Fences used to enclose a lot can be up to 4 feet on the lot line. For the Wilsons' project, we designed the fence to sit just inside the setback line at the maximum 6-foot height — giving them full privacy without running afoul of local ordinances.

The Village of Lake Orion requires a separate fence permit for new construction. We handled all of that paperwork for the Wilsons. It's one of those things that sounds simple but can trip up homeowners who try to go it alone. Miss the permit, and you could be looking at fines or even having to tear down a fence that's already built.

### Step 2: Post Hole Digging and Setting

With the layout marked and permits in hand, we moved to the most critical phase of the installation: the post holes.

In Michigan, fence posts need to be set below the frost line — typically 42 to 48 inches deep. That's 3.5 to 4 feet into the ground. If you skimp on post depth, frost heave will push your posts up over the winter, and by spring you'll have a crooked, leaning fence.

We dug all 280 linear feet of post holes using a power auger, carefully avoiding underground utilities (Miss Dig was called well in advance). For this project, we set posts every 8 feet — standard spacing for a 6-foot privacy fence with board-on-board pickets.

Each post was set in concrete, with the concrete mounded slightly above grade to direct water away from the post base. This is a small detail that makes a huge difference in cedar post longevity. Water pooling around the base of a post is the fastest way to start rot, even with naturally rot-resistant cedar.

### Step 3: Installing Rails and Pickets

Once the concrete cured (we gave it a full 48 hours in the June heat), we moved to framing. We attached 2×4 pressure-treated top and bottom rails between the posts. Using pressure-treated lumber for the rails — even with cedar posts and pickets — is standard practice. The rails are hidden behind the cedar pickets, and pressure-treated lumber provides extra structural strength at a lower cost.

Then came the board-on-board picket installation. Each 6-foot cedar picket was individually nailed to the rails, alternating from one side to the other. This is slower than standard fence construction — you're essentially running two layers of pickets instead of one — but the result is worth it.

The Wilsons' fence required approximately 560 pickets for the full 280-foot run. That's a lot of lumber, but it creates a fence that's solid as a wall.

### Step 4: Gate Installation

The Wilsons needed three gates: two 4-foot walk gates (one for the side access, one for the back garden) and a 10-foot double-drive gate wide enough for their SUV and a boat trailer.

The walk gates were framed with cedar and fitted with heavy-duty gate hinges and self-closing latches. For the double-drive gate, we used steel posts with adjustable gate hinges — these gates need extra structural support because they span a wide opening and get frequent use.

We also installed a driveway gate drop rod — a simple metal pin that drops into a ground sleeve to hold the gate steady when open. It's a small detail, but it prevents the gate from swinging in the wind and straining the hinges over time.

### Step 5: Final Touches

After all the pickets were installed, we trimmed the tops of the posts with post caps — a cedar pyramid cap that gives the fence a finished, professional look. We also checked every single nail and screw, tightened hardware, and did a full walk-through with the Wilsons.

The total installation took four days with a crew of three. That included the post hole digging, concrete work, framing, picket installation, and gates. The June weather cooperated — sunny and 80 degrees — which meant the concrete cured quickly and the crew could work efficiently.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Custom Cedar Fences in Lake Orion

### How much does a cedar privacy fence cost in Lake Orion, Michigan?

For a professionally installed Western red cedar board-on-board privacy fence, expect to pay between $35 and $50 per linear foot, depending on site conditions, linear footage, gate requirements, and grading complexity. A typical 200-foot installation runs $7,000 to $10,000. We provide free, detailed estimates for every property.

### Do I need a permit for a fence in Orion Township?

Orion Township does not require a building permit for fences, but zoning regulations apply. Privacy fences cannot exceed 6 feet in height and must be located outside the required yard setback area. Fences on the lot line are limited to 4 feet unless a variance is obtained. The Village of Lake Orion requires a separate fence permit. BF Fence handles all permitting for every project we build.

### How long does a cedar fence last in Michigan?

A properly built Western red cedar fence with posts set below the frost line and concrete footings will last 20 to 25 years in Michigan's climate. Occasional sealing or staining every 3 to 5 years will extend the life and maintain the color. Even untreated, cedar's natural resistance to rot and insects means it will outperform pressure-treated pine by a significant margin.

### Does a custom cedar fence add property value?

Yes. Professionally installed cedar fences typically return 50 to 70 percent of their cost in added property value. Homes with fenced yards sell approximately 18 days faster than comparable unfenced properties, according to the National Association of Realtors. In family-oriented neighborhoods like Lake Orion, a high-quality fence is a major selling point.

### What's the best time of year to install a fence in Michigan?

Spring (April through June) and fall (September through October) are ideal. The ground is workable without being frozen, temperatures are comfortable for the crew, and the concrete has time to cure properly. Summer installations are fine but can be harder on the crew and the lawn. We install year-round, weather permitting.

### How is board-on-board different from standard privacy fence construction?

In standard privacy fence construction, pickets are attached to one side of the rails with small gaps between them. As the wood shrinks over time, these gaps widen and privacy is reduced. Board-on-board construction staggers pickets on alternating sides of the rails, creating a solid barrier with no gaps. It also looks finished from both sides — equally important for a corner lot where the fence is visible from the street.

### Can you match an existing fence or HOA requirements?

Absolutely. We can match fence styles, heights, and wood types to blend with existing fencing on your property or meet HOA Covenants, Conditions, and Restrictions (CC&Rs). Always check your HOA documents before starting a fence project. We can review them with you during your free estimate.

### What about gates — can you build custom sizes?

Yes. We build custom gates in any size and configuration. Single walk gates, double-drive gates, gates for boat or RV access — we've built them all. For the Lake Orion project, we installed a 10-foot drive gate that accommodates both a family SUV and a boat trailer, which is a common need in lake communities.
